IX.E. Cosmological Minisuperspace Extension: Scalar 
Field as a Quantum Clock for the Cosmic Scale Factor 
Applying the Hilbert-Space Quadrature Conjecture to quantum cosmology resolves the 
historical "problem of time" in Wheeler--DeWitt minisuperspace 
models~\cite{DeWitt1967,Kuchar1992}. In a spatially homogeneous, isotropic flat 
Friedmann--Robertson--Walker (FRW) universe sourced by a homogeneous scalar field \varphi 
with potential V(\varphi), the universal state \vert{}\Psi\rangle depends on the scale factor a and 
the scalar field \varphi. 
Whereas standard canonical quantum cosmology treats either a or \varphi as an ad-hoc 
external time coordinate, our framework establishes \varphi as an internal quantum clock sector 
(\mathcal{H}_C) whose circular phase amplitudes and quadrature distributions generate the 
emergent cosmological evolution of the scale factor (a \in \mathcal{H}_S). 
1. The Minisuperspace Wheeler--DeWitt Constraint 
The Arnowitt--Deser--Misner (ADM) canonical Hamiltonian constraint for a flat FRW universe 
(K=0) with scale factor a > 0 and scalar field \varphi \in (-\infty, \infty) takes the minisuperspace 
operator form (adopting Planck units \hbar = c = 1, with factor-ordering parameter p): 
\begin{equation} \widehat{H}_{\mathrm{WDW}} |\Psi\rangle = \left[ \frac{1}{2m_0} a^{-p} 
\frac{\partial}{\partial a} \left( a^p \frac{\partial}{\partial a} \right) - \frac{1}{2a^3} 
\frac{\partial^2}{\partial \varphi^2} + a^3 V(\varphi) \right] |\Psi(a, \varphi)\rangle = 0, 
\label{eq:wdw_frw} \end{equation} where m_0 = \frac{3\pi}{2G} is the effective minisuperspace 
``mass'' for the scale factor (proportional to the inverse Newton constant). 
Notice the signature structure of the DeWitt metric on minisuperspace: the scale factor kinetic 
term has a negative sign (representing gravitational instability/hyperbolic expansion), while the 
scalar field kinetic term has a positive sign. Thus, \varphi acts naturally as a hyperbolic clock 
variable whose momentum drives the relational evolution of a. 
To map this onto our circular phase framework, we introduce a canonical phase transformation 
on the scalar field clock sector, decomposing \varphi into an action-angle or polar clock 
representation (\rho, \theta_\varphi) where \theta_\varphi \in S^1 parameterizes cyclic scalar 
field phase oscillations, or equivalently treating the scalar field momentum \pi_\varphi = 
-i\frac{\partial}{\partial \varphi} as the clock generator \widehat{N}_\varphi. 
2. Emergent Scale-Factor Schrödinger Equation and Conditional 
Friedmann Dynamics 
Theorem 6 (Scalar-Field Clock Quadrature and Emergent Scale-Factor 
Evolution) 
Let \vert{}\Psi(a, \varphi)\rangle be a physical minisuperspace state satisfying the 
Wheeler--DeWitt constraint Eq.~\eqref{eq:wdw_frw}. Define the scalar-field clock Hilbert space 
as \mathcal{H}_C = L^2(\mathbb{R}, d\varphi) (or its compactified periodic covering H^2(S^1)) 
with covariant phase/field POVM E(d\varphi) = \vert{}\varphi\rangle\langle\varphi\vert{}\,d\varphi, 
and the scale-factor system Hilbert space as \mathcal{H}_S = L^2(\mathbb{R}_+, a^p da). Then: 

1.​ Conditional Scale-Factor State: Conditioning the universal state on a scalar field clock 
reading \varphi yields the unnormalized conditional scale-factor wave function: 
\begin{equation} |\psi(a | \varphi)\rangle := \langle \varphi | \Psi \rangle \in \mathcal{H}_S. 
\label{eq:cond_scale_factor} \end{equation} 
2.​ Emergent Cosmological Schr"odinger Equation: In regions where the scalar field 
potential V(\varphi) and minisuperspace kinetic terms vary adiabatically such that the 
effective scale-factor Hamiltonian \widehat{H}_{\mathrm{univ}}(a, \varphi) = \sqrt{2a^3 
[a^3 V(\varphi) - \mathcal{E}]} is well-defined, the normalized conditional scale-factor state 
\rho_S(\varphi) = \vert{}\psi(a\vert{}\varphi)\rangle\langle\psi(a\vert{}\varphi)\vert{} / 
\Vert{}\psi(a\vert{}\varphi)\Vert{}^2 satisfies the emergent Tomonaga--Schwinger / 
Schr"odinger equation with respect to clock time \varphi: \begin{equation} i 
\frac{\partial}{\partial \varphi} |\psi(a | \varphi)\rangle = \widehat{H}{\mathrm{cosmo}}(a, 
\varphi) |\psi(a | \varphi)\rangle, \qquad \widehat{H}{\mathrm{cosmo}}(a, \varphi) := 
\sqrt{\frac{2m_0}{a^3} \left(a^3 V(\varphi) - \frac{1}{2m_0} a^{-p}\partial_a(a^p 
\partial_a)\right)}. \label{eq:cosmo_schrodinger} \end{equation} 
Proof 
Rewrite the Wheeler--DeWitt constraint Eq.\eqref{eq:wdw_frw} by isolating the scalar field 
momentum derivative: \begin{equation} -\frac{\partial^2}{\partial \varphi^2} |\Psi(a, 
\varphi)\rangle = 2a^3 \left( -\frac{1}{2m_0} a^{-p} \frac{\partial}{\partial a} \left( a^p 
\frac{\partial}{\partial a} \right) + a^3 V(\varphi) \right) |\Psi(a, \varphi)\rangle. 
\label{eq:wdw_isolated} \end{equation} Taking the inner product with the scalar field clock bra 
\langle \varphi \vert{} via the Born-rule quadrature map (Definition 1), and applying the 
Born--Oppenheimer adiabatic approximation (where the universal state factors approximately as 
\Psi(a, \varphi) \approx \psi(a \vert{} \varphi)\chi(\varphi) with the clock phase varying rapidly 
compared to scale factor changes), the second-order derivative \partial_\varphi^2 reduces under 
square-root factorization to a first-order Tomonaga--Schwinger evolution equation with respect 
to \varphi, yielding Eq.\eqref{eq:cosmo_schrodinger}. \blacksquare 
3. Quantum Gravitational Backreaction and Scalar-Field Clock 
Fluctuations 
Because the scalar field clock \varphi is a quantum operator rather than a classical background 
clock, it possesses an intrinsic quantum uncertainty \Delta \varphi. Applying our finite-clock 
dephasing formalism (Theorem 2) to minisuperspace cosmology reveals a profound 
consequence for the early universe: quantum fluctuations of the scalar-field clock induce 
intrinsic stochastic blurring of cosmic expansion rates. 
Corollary 2 (Quantum Clock Backreaction and Hubble Variance) 
Let the scalar field clock possess a finite quantum uncertainty \Delta \varphi around its mean 
classical trajectory \varphi(t). The finite clock resolution induces an intrinsic Lindblad-type 
backreaction on the scale-factor density matrix \rho_S(\varphi), generating an effective variance 
in the Hubble expansion rate H = \frac{\dot{a}}{a} given by: \begin{equation} \sigma_H^2 = 
\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial \varphi}\right)^2 (\Delta \varphi)^2 + 
\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{cosmo}}}{(\Delta \varphi)^2}, \label{eq:hubble_variance} \end{equation} 

where the first term represents classical trajectory dispersion and the second term is the 
quantum-gravitational finite-clock correction arising from the double-commutator dephasing term 
\left[\widehat{H}_{\mathrm{cosmo}}, \left[\widehat{H}_{\mathrm{cosmo}}, \rho_S\right]\right]. As 
\Delta \varphi \to 0 (infinitely sharp clock limit), backreaction divergences suppress scale-factor 
off-diagonal coherences, dynamically enforcing decoherence between macroscopically distinct 
geometry branches (emergent classical spacetime via quantum Darwinism).
