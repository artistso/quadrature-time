IX.G. The Hartle--Hawking No-Boundary Wave 
Function via Covariant Phase POVMs 
Applying the Hilbert-Space Quadrature Conjecture to quantum cosmology provides a rigorous 
operational foundation for the Hartle--Hawking ``no-boundary'' 
proposal~\cite{HartleHawking1983}. In the standard formulation, the no-boundary wave function 
\Psi_{\text{HH}}[h_{ij}, \varphi] is defined by a Euclidean path integral over compact 
4-geometries g and regular field configurations \phi bounded only by the 3-metric h_{ij} and field 
\varphi on the spatial hypersurface: \begin{equation} \Psi_{\text{HH}}[h_{ij}, \varphi] = 
\int_{\partial g = (h, \varphi)} \mathcal{D}g , \mathcal{D}\phi , \exp\left(-I_E[g, \phi]\right). 
\label{eq:hh_path_integral} \end{equation} Historically, interpreting Euclidean time \tau_E and its 
transition to Lorentzian spacetime required formal analytic continuations (Wick rotations) of the 
metric signature. Within our framework, Euclidean time is the circular phase parameter 
\theta_E \in S^1_E of an internal Euclidean clock sector \mathcal{H}_{C,E}, and the 
Hartle--Hawking wave function itself is a state-generated quadrature projection of a timeless 
boundary-free universal state onto the covariant phase POVM E(d\theta_E). 
1. The Euclidean Clock Circle and No-Boundary State Generation 
Let the Euclidean proper time \tau_E along compact regular caps be compactified or integrated 
over a Euclidean circle S^1_E of fundamental circumference \beta = 2\pi/\omega_E. The 
Euclidean clock Hilbert space is \mathcal{H}_{C,E} = L^2(S^1_E, d\theta_E/2\pi), equipped with 
the covariant Holevo phase POVM: \begin{equation} E_E(d\theta_E) = \frac{1}{2\pi} 
|\theta_E)(\theta_E| , d\theta_E, \qquad |\theta_E) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} e^{in\theta_E} 
|n\rangle_E. \label{eq:euclidean_povm} \end{equation} 
Theorem 8 (Quadrature Formulation of the Hartle--Hawking 
No-Boundary State) 
Let \mathcal{H}_U = \mathcal{H}_{C,E} \otimes \mathcal{H}_{\text{geom}} be the bipartite 
universe comprising the Euclidean clock sector and the gravitational superspace geometry. The 
Hartle--Hawking no-boundary state \vert{}\Psi_{\text{NB}}\rangle \in \mathcal{H}_U is generated 
by integrating the covariant Euclidean phase POVM E_E(d\theta_E) over the compact circle 
S^1_E anchored at a smooth, zero-volume regular origin (\vert{}\text{cap}\rangle): 
\vert{}\Psi_{\text{NB}}\rangle = \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{d\theta_E}{2\pi} \, \vert{}\theta_E)_C \otimes 
\left( \int_{\text{cap}}^{\theta_E} \mathcal{D}g\,\mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{-I_E[g,\phi]} 
\vert{}\text{geom}(\theta_E)\rangle \right). 
The Hartle--Hawking wave function \Psi_{\text{HH}}[h_{ij}, \varphi] is the exact conditional 
quadrature amplitude obtained by projecting \vert{}\Psi_{\text{NB}}\rangle onto the covariant 
phase state and the spatial metric eigenstate: 
\Psi_{\text{HH}}[h_{ij}, \varphi; \theta_E] = \langle \theta_E, h_{ij}, \varphi \vert{} \Psi_{\text{NB}} 
\rangle. 
Proof 

By definition, the Hartle--Hawking path integral sums over all compact Euclidean geometries 
starting from a point (zero 3-volume h_{ij} \to 0 at the origin). In our relational framework, the 
"radius" from the smooth cap acts as the Euclidean clock angle \theta_E \in [0, 2\pi) winding 
around the Euclidean proper-time circle. 
The Euclidean action I_E[g, \phi] acts as the generator of translations around S^1_E. Applying 
the covariant phase POVM E_E(d\theta_E) of Proposition 1 to the superspace state 
decomposes the no-boundary path integral into a continuous superposition of conditional 
geometry states \vert{}\psi(\theta_E)\rangle = \int \mathcal{D}g\,\mathcal{D}\phi \, e^{-I_E[g,\phi]} 
\vert{}\text{geom}(\theta_E)\rangle weighted by the Euclidean clock phase amplitudes. 
Projecting this state onto the metric and scalar field configuration (h_{ij}, \varphi) yields 
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hh_path_integral}, establishing that the Hartle--Hawking wave function is a 
state-generated quadrature amplitude over the Euclidean circular phase fiber. \blacksquare 
2. Analytic Continuation to Lorentzian Quadrature Time 
To recover Lorentzian cosmology (oscillatory De Sitter or FRW expansion) from the Euclidean 
no-boundary wave function, standard treatments perform a Wick rotation \theta_E = i\theta_L + 
\frac{\pi}{2} (or \tau_E = i t). 
Within the Hilbert-Space Quadrature Conjecture, this analytic continuation is not an ad-hoc 
mathematical trick, but the transition from the Euclidean compact phase fiber S^1_E to the 
physical universal cover time \mathbb{R} via the winding quotient map (\Pi_\omega): 
1.​ Euclidean Phase Regime (\theta_E \in S^1_E): The Euclidean clock POVM 
E_E(d\theta_E) measures compact, periodic Euclidean histories (the "cap"). The 
quadrature distribution M_{\text{NB}}(\theta_E) = \Vert{}\Psi_{\text{NB}}(\theta_E)\Vert{}^2 
is regular and bounded at the origin (\theta_E = 0). 
2.​ Wick-Rotation Boundary Transition: At the equator of the no-boundary manifold 
(\theta_E = \pi/2), the Euclidean metric signature transitions to Lorentzian signature. In our 
framework, this corresponds to the phase transition where the compact Euclidean clock 
Hardy space H^2(S^1_E) unwraps into the universal cover clock Hilbert space 
\mathcal{H}_C^{\text{univ}} = L^2(\mathbb{R}, dt) governed by the group-averaged 
constraint \mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{phys}} = \delta(\widehat{C}_{\text{univ}}) (Section II). 
3.​ Emergent Lorentzian Dynamics: Once analytically continued across the equator, the 
conditional state satisfies the Lorentzian Wheeler--DeWitt / Schr"odinger equation 
(Theorem 6), and the squared quadrature distribution Q_\Psi(\theta_L, a) = \vert{}\langle 
\theta_L, a \vert{} \Psi_{\text{Lorentz}} \rangle\vert{}^2 generates real-time cosmological 
expansion.
