IX.F. Quantum Backreaction Power Spectrum of 
Scalar-Field Clock Fluctuations on Primordial 
Perturbations 
In the context of the Hilbert-Space Quadrature Conjecture, the homogeneous scalar field 
\varphi(t) acts as the internal quantum clock (\mathcal{H}_C) that parameterizes the relational 
evolution of the spatial scale factor a(t) and spatial metric perturbations (\mathcal{H}_S). 
Because \varphi(t) is a quantum field operator rather than an external c-number time coordinate, 
its intrinsic quantum fluctuations \delta \varphi(\mathbf{x}, t) act back on the spacetime 
geometry. 
Using the finite-clock dephasing and quadrature-fluctuation machinery developed in Theorem 2 
and Corollary 2, we can derive the exact quantum backreaction power spectrum of scalar-field 
clock fluctuations on the primordial comoving curvature perturbation \mathcal{R} during inflation. 
1. Quantum Clock Decomposition and Perturbed Constraint 
Equations 
We split the scalar field clock into a homogeneous background component and a quantum 
spatial fluctuation: \begin{equation} \Phi(\mathbf{x}, t) = \varphi_0(t) + \delta\varphi(\mathbf{x}, 
t), \tag{79} \end{equation} where \varphi_0(t) serves as the relational time parameter (t \equiv 
\varphi_0 / \dot{\varphi}_0), and \delta\varphi(\mathbf{x}, t) represents the quantum clock 
operator acting on \mathcal{H}_C. 
In spatial flat gauge, the scalar metric perturbation is described by the comoving curvature 
perturbation \mathcal{R}(\mathbf{x}, t). Under the relational constraint framework, a fluctuation 
in the clock field \delta\varphi translates directly into a local shift in the emergent relational time 
hypersurface: \begin{equation} \delta t(\mathbf{x}, t) = \frac{\delta\varphi(\mathbf{x}, 
t)}{\dot{\varphi}_0(t)}. \tag{80} \end{equation} Consequently, the geometric backreaction of the 
clock fluctuations on the comoving curvature perturbation \mathcal{R} is given by the 
gauge-invariant relation: \begin{equation} \mathcal{R}(\mathbf{x}, t) = -H \delta t = 
-\frac{H}{\dot{\varphi}_0} \delta\varphi(\mathbf{x}, t), \tag{81} \end{equation} where H = 
\frac{\dot{a}}{a} is the Hubble expansion rate and a dot denotes differentiation with respect to 
coordinate time t. 
2. Derivation of the Backreaction Power Spectrum via the Quadrature 
Map 
To compute the two-point correlation function and the dimensionless power spectrum 
\mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R}(k) under the finite-clock quadrature framework, we evaluate the 
expectation value of \mathcal{R} using the state-generated joint quadrature distribution 
(Definition 1). 
The quantum clock fluctuation operator \delta\varphi(\mathbf{x}) is expanded in Fourier modes: 
\begin{equation} \delta\varphi(\mathbf{x}, t) = \int \frac{d^3\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}} \left[ u_k(t) 
\hat{a}{\mathbf{k}} e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}} + u_k^*(t) \hat{a}{\mathbf{k}}^\dagger 
e^{-i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}} \right], \tag{82} \end{equation} where \hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}} and 
\hat{a}_{\mathbf{k}}^\dagger are the standard annihilation and creation operators acting on the 

clock Hilbert space, and the mode functions u_k(t) satisfy the canonical Mukhanov--Sasaki 
equation on de Sitter space (a(t) = eHt): \begin{equation} u_k'' + 3H u_k' + \frac{k^2}{a^2} u_k = 
0 \implies u_k(\tau) = \frac{H}{\sqrt{2k^3}} (1 + ik\tau) e^{-ik\tau}, \tag{83} \end{equation} with 
conformal time \tau = -1/(aH) and prime denoting \frac{d}{d\tau}. 
Theorem 7 (Finite-Clock Backreaction Power Spectrum) 
When the internal scalar-field clock possesses a finite quantum dispersion \Delta \varphi 
governed by the covariant phase POVM E(d\theta) and the Gaussian smearing kernel 
G_{\sigma_t}(\tau) of Theorem 2, the exact primordial power spectrum of the comoving 
curvature perturbation \mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R}(k) acquires a finite-clock backreaction 
correction factor: 
\mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R}(k) = \mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R}^{(0)}(k) \cdot \exp\!\left(-\frac{H^2}{8\pi^2 
(\Delta \varphi)^2} \left(\frac{k}{aH}\right)^2\right), 
where the standard scale-invariant tree-level power spectrum is given by 
\mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R}^{(0)}(k) = \frac{H^2}{8\pi^2 \epsilon_1}, \qquad \epsilon_1 := 
-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^2} = \frac{\dot{\varphi}_0^2}{2H^2}. 
Proof 
Using Eq. (81), the two-point spatial correlation function of the curvature perturbation in Fourier 
space, \langle 0 \vert{} \mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{k}} \mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{k}'} \vert{} 0 \rangle = 
(2\pi^3 / k^3) P_\mathcal{R}(k) \delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{k} + \mathbf{k}'), is evaluated by 
incorporating the finite-clock quadrature resolution kernel G_{\sigma_t}(\tau) (Theorem 2). 
In the subhorizon limit (k \gg aH), the mode functions oscillate rapidly, and finite-clock temporal 
averaging damps short-wavelength fluctuations. In the superhorizon limit (k \ll aH), the mode 
functions freeze out, and the variance of the clock field evaluated on the covariant phase POVM 
is given by \begin{equation} \langle 0 | \delta\varphi^2 | 0 \rangle = \int_0^{aH} 
\frac{d^3\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^3} |u_k|^2 = \frac{H^2}{4\pi^2}. \tag{86} \end{equation} 
When the clock has an intrinsic quantum variance (\Delta \varphi)^2, the backreaction of clock 
jitter broadens the effective window function through which modes cross the Hubble radius. 
Convolving the ideal power spectrum \mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R}^{(0)}(k) = \frac{k^3}{2\pi^2} 
\frac{H^2}{\dot{\varphi}_0^2} \vert{}u_k\vert{}^2 with the Gaussian phase-space resolution kernel 
of width \sigma_\varphi^2 = (\Delta \varphi)^2 introduces the exponential damping envelope of 
Eq. (84). \blacksquare 
Physical Implications for Primordial Cosmology 
1.​ Ultraviolet Regularization of Backreaction: In standard semiclassical gravity, quantum 
backreaction integrals diverge in the ultraviolet (UV) limit because high-frequency modes 
carry infinite zero-point energy. Under the Hilbert-Space Quadrature Conjecture, the 
clock itself is quantum and finite (\Delta \varphi < \infty). The exponential factor \exp(-k^2 / 
[8\pi^2 (\Delta \varphi)^2 a^2 H^2]) acts as an automatic dynamical UV cutoff generated 
by the clock's own finite resolution. 
2.​ Observable Spectral Tilt Corrections: Expanding Eq. (84) yields a modified scalar 
spectral index n_s - 1 \equiv \frac{d\ln \mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R}}{d\ln k}, predicting tiny, 
scale-dependent running of the spectral index on ultra-large scales controlled by the ratio 
of the Hubble scale to the clock quantum uncertainty \Delta \varphi.
