IX.H. Near-Horizon Limit (r_C \to r_s^+), 
Bisognano--Wichmann Theorem, and the 
Hawking--Unruh Temperature 
Completing the near-horizon limit (r_C \to r_s^+) of our curved-spacetime extension reveals a 
deep connection between the Hilbert-Space Quadrature Conjecture, Tomita--Takesaki 
modular flow (Proposition 2), and black hole thermodynamics. 
As a quantum clock approaches the event horizon of a Schwarzschild black hole, the 
gravitational redshift diverges, compressing the local proper time of the clock relative to 
asymptotic coordinate time t. We show how the Quadrature Time modular operator \Delta_\Psi 
near the horizon maps directly onto the Bisognano--Wichmann boost generator, reproducing the 
Hawking--Unruh temperature T_H = \frac{\hbar \kappa}{2\pi k_B} purely from internal 
state-generated quadrature flow. 
1. Near-Horizon Rindler Approximation 
Near the Schwarzschild event horizon r = r_s, introduce the proper radial distance coordinate 
\rho defined by d\rho = \left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right)^{-1/2} dr, such that very close to the horizon (r - 
r_s \ll r_s), 
\rho \approx 2 r_s \sqrt{1 - \frac{r_s}{r}} \implies N(r) = \sqrt{1 - \frac{r_s}{r}} \approx \frac{\rho}{2 
r_s} = \frac{\kappa \rho}{c^2}, 
where \kappa = \frac{c^4}{4 G M_G} is the black hole surface gravity. 
Substituting N(r) \approx \kappa \rho / c^2 into the Schwarzschild metric (Eq. 55) transforms the 
near-horizon geometry into Rindler spacetime: 
ds^2 \approx \left(\frac{\kappa \rho}{c^2}\right)^2 c^2 dt^2 - d\rho^2 - r_s^2(d\theta^2 + 
\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2). 
Defining Rindler acceleration units by setting \eta = \kappa t / c and x = \rho, the metric takes 
the conformally flat Minkowski wedge form: 
ds^2 \approx -dx^2 + x^2 d\eta^2 - r_s^2 d\Omega^2. 
2. Bisognano--Wichmann Theorem and Near-Horizon Modular Flow 
In algebraic quantum field theory, the Bisognano--Wichmann theorem states that for any local 
quantum field theory restricted to the Rindler wedge (x > 0), the modular operator \Delta_\psi 
associated with the vacuum state \psi is generated by the Lorentz boost operator K that 
preserves the wedge: 
\Delta_\psi^{is} = e^{-i s K}, 
where s is the modular flow parameter. 
Within our framework (Proposition 2), the universal state \vert{}\Psi\rangle restricted to the 
near-horizon wedge induces a local reduced density operator \rho_{\text{wedge}} = 
\operatorname{Tr}_{\text{exterior}} \vert{}\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi\vert{}. Because the modular 
Hamiltonian is K = -\ln \rho_{\text{wedge}}, the modular flow \sigma_s^\Psi(A) = \Delta_\psi^{is} 
A \Delta_\psi^{-is} generates local time translations along the Rindler trajectory \eta = \kappa t. 
Theorem 9 (Quadrature Modular Flow and Hawking--Unruh 

Temperature) 
Let a quantum clock \mathcal{H}_C be stationed in the near-horizon region at proper distance 
\rho \ll r_s, and let the universal state \vert{}\Psi\rangle be globally stationary under the 
Wheeler--DeWitt constraint. Then: 
1.​ Modular Thermal KMS Condition: The state of the quantum fields restricted to the 
near-horizon Rindler wedge satisfies the Kubo--Martin--Schwinger (KMS) thermal 
condition with respect to modular flow parameter s with inverse modular temperature 
\beta_{\text{mod}} = 2\pi. 
2.​ Emergence of Hawking--Unruh Temperature: Mapping the modular parameter s to 
asymptotic coordinate time t via the Quadrature Time relation t = -\hbar \beta s yields an 
effective thermal state at the Hawking--Unruh temperature: 
T_H = \frac{\hbar \kappa}{2\pi k_B c} = \frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi G M_G k_B}. 
1.​ Divergence of Near-Horizon Lindblad Dephasing: Substituting the near-horizon lapse 
N(r_C) \approx \frac{\kappa \rho}{c^2} into Corollary 1 shows that as \rho \to 0 (r_C \to 
r_s^+), the coordinate-time Lindblad dephasing rate diverges as 
\Gamma_{jk}^{\text{grav}}(r_S, r_C) \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}} \longrightarrow \infty, 
reflecting the infinite thermal fluctuation blueshift experienced by any physical clock stationed 
arbitrarily close to the event horizon. 
Proof 
By the Tomita--Takesaki modular theory (Section VI), any faithful state on a von Neumann 
algebra satisfies the KMS condition under modular automorphism \sigma_s^\Psi with period 
\Delta s = 2\pi. In Rindler spacetime, the boost generator K is related to Killing time t by K = 
\frac{c^3}{\hbar \kappa} \widehat{H}_{\text{Killing}}. Equating the modular periodicity \Delta s = 
2\pi to physical time evolution via the identification t = -\hbar \beta s (Proposition 2) gives 
\Delta t = \frac{2\pi \hbar}{\kappa} = \frac{1}{k_B T_H} \implies T_H = \frac{\hbar \kappa}{2\pi 
k_B}, 
proving the Hawking--Unruh temperature emerges directly from the modular quadrature 
condition of the universal state \vert{}\Psi\rangle. 
Furthermore, substituting N(r_C) \approx \frac{\kappa \rho}{c^2} into the curved-spacetime 
Lindblad rate of Eq. (70) yields 
\Gamma_{jk}^{\text{grav}}(r_S, r_C) = \frac{N(r_S)^2}{\sqrt{\frac{\kappa \rho}{c^2}}} 
\frac{(E_j^{\text{loc}} - E_k^{\text{loc}})^2}{2\hbar \Delta E_C^{\text{loc}}} \propto 
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}, 
which diverges as \rho \to 0, proving that near the event horizon, quantum clock fluctuations are 
infinitely blueshifted by the local curvature geometry. \blacksquare
