1. The Normalization Singularity in \mathcal{H}_C \otimes 
\mathcal{H}_S 
In Sections 2 and 6, the clock Hilbert space is defined as \mathcal{H}_C = L^2(S^1, d\theta/2\pi) 
with a discrete integer spectrum N\vert{}n\rangle = n\vert{}n\rangle (n \in \mathbb{Z}), while the 
global constraint is imposed as: 
\widehat{C}\vert{}\Psi\rangle = \left(\hbar \omega N \otimes I_S + I_C \otimes 
\widehat{H}_S\right)\vert{}\Psi\rangle = 0 
For this equation to possess non-trivial solutions \vert{}\Psi\rangle \neq 0, the spectrum of 
\widehat{H}_S must intersect the spectrum of -\hbar\omega N. Because N has strictly discrete 
eigenvalues n \in \mathbb{Z}, a solution only exists if the system Hamiltonian \widehat{H}_S has 
eigenvalues that are exact integer multiples of \hbar\omega: 
E_k = -n_k \hbar \omega 
If \widehat{H}_S has a continuous spectrum or incommensurate energy gaps, \ker(\widehat{C}) 
= \{0\} in the strict Hilbert space \mathcal{H}_C \otimes \mathcal{H}_S. Furthermore, if \theta \in 
S^1 is strictly periodic, any solution \vert{}\Psi(\theta)\rangle = e^{-i\widehat{H}_S \theta / 
\hbar\omega}\vert{}\Psi(0)\rangle must satisfy the boundary condition \vert{}\Psi(2\pi)\rangle = 
\vert{}\Psi(0)\rangle, which again forces E_k / \hbar\omega \in \mathbb{Z}. 
How to resolve this in Section 1 of the paper: 
●​ Option A (Rigged Hilbert Space / Gel'fand Triple): Allow the clock phase to unwrap 
onto the universal cover \mathbb{R} via a Gel'fand triple \Phi \subset \mathcal{H}_U 
\subset \Phi^\times, where physical states \vert{}\Psi\rangle \in \Phi^\times are generalized 
eigenstates (like plane waves) normalized via group-averaging (refined algebraic 
quantization). 
●​ Option B (Approximate Constraint / Spectral Window): Keep \mathcal{H}_C = 
L^2(S^1) strictly compact, and replace the exact constraint \widehat{C}\vert{}\Psi\rangle = 
0 with a physical subspace projection \vert{}\widehat{C}\vert{}\Psi\rangle\vert{} \le \epsilon, 
or use the finite-dimensional Pegg-Barnett / Salecker-Wigner clock from Section 14 as the 
fundamental definition rather than an approximation. 
2. Covariant Phase POVM and Pauli's Theorem 
In Section 2, the states \vert{}\theta\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{n \in \mathbb{Z}} 
e^{in\theta}\vert{}n\rangle are orthogonal distributions when n runs from -\infty to +\infty. 
However, a clock whose generator N is unbounded below (n \to -\infty) means the clock energy 
\hbar\omega N has no ground state. 
If you restrict the clock to have a physical ground state (n \ge 0, placing it in the Hardy space 
H^2(S^1)), the states: 
\vert{}\theta\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} e^{in\theta}\vert{}n\rangle 
are no longer orthogonal (\langle \theta \vert{} \theta' \rangle \neq \delta_{2\pi}(\theta - \theta')). 

Instead, they form an overcomplete frame that resolves the identity as a covariant phase 
POVM (the Holevo phase observable): 
E(d\theta) = \vert{}\theta\rangle\langle\theta\vert{} \frac{d\theta}{2\pi}, \qquad \int_0^{2\pi} 
E(d\theta) = I_C 
Making this explicit immediately satisfies Step 1 of Section 22: it explains why E(d\theta) is a 
true POVM rather than a projection-valued measure (PVM), naturally circumvents Pauli's 
theorem with a lower-bounded clock Hamiltonian (n \ge 0), and introduces an intrinsic quantum 
phase blur straight from the semi-boundedness of energy. 
3. Conditional Normalization vs. Raw Quadrature 
There is a subtle tension between Section 4 (where Q_\Psi(\theta) = 
\Vert{}\langle\theta\vert{}\Psi\rangle\Vert{}^2 is a non-trivial probability distribution over the circle) 
and Section 6 (where \vert{}\Psi(\theta)\rangle obeys the unitary Schrödinger equation). 
If \vert{}\Psi(\theta)\rangle obeys the exact Schrödinger equation: 
i\hbar\omega \frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} \vert{}\Psi(\theta)\rangle = \widehat{H}_S 
\vert{}\Psi(\theta)\rangle 
and \widehat{H}_S is self-adjoint, then the norm of \vert{}\Psi(\theta)\rangle is strictly constant 
across the entire circle: 
\frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} Q_\Psi(\theta) = \frac{\partial}{\partial \theta} 
\langle\Psi(\theta)\vert{}\Psi(\theta)\rangle = 0 \implies Q_\Psi(\theta) = \frac{1}{2\pi} 
In the ideal Page-Wootters limit, the marginal distribution Q_\Psi(\theta) of the clock is 
completely uniform—all moments of time are equally probable globally. The non-trivial 
dynamics reside in the joint system-clock quadrature: 
Q_\Psi(\theta, a) = \vert{}\langle \theta, a \vert{} \Psi \rangle\vert{}^2 = p_\Psi(\theta) \, 
\operatorname{Tr}_S\left[\rho_S(\theta) \Pi_a\right] 
where \Pi_a = \vert{}a\rangle\langle a\vert{} is a system observable projector. If you want 
Q_\Psi(\theta) itself to be a peaked wavepacket that tracks a semiclassical parameter (as in 
Section 5 and Section 16), \vert{}\Psi\rangle cannot be an exact zero-eigenstate of \widehat{C}; 
instead, it must be a coherent wavepacket state evolving with respect to an auxiliary parameter, 
or \widehat{C} must include a clock potential term. Distinguishing between the uniform global 
clock marginal (stationary history) and peaked conditional system expectation values will 
make the formalism bulletproof against standard critiques of the Page-Wootters mechanism. 
4. Exact Master Equation for Finite-Clock Lindblad Decoherence 
To execute Step 3 of Section 22 (quantifying finite-clock corrections), when the clock state has 
a finite energy spread \Delta E_C (or when using a non-orthogonal covariant POVM E(d\theta) 
on a semi-bounded spectrum), conditioning on \theta yields a convolution of unitary evolutions. 
Expanding to second order in the temporal uncertainty \sigma_t \sim \hbar / (2\Delta E_C) 
transforms the exact Schrödinger equation into a Lindblad-type master equation in the 
energy basis: 
\frac{d\rho_S(t)}{dt} = -\frac{i}{\hbar}[\widehat{H}_S, \rho_S(t)] - 
\frac{\gamma_{\text{clock}}}{\hbar^2} \left[\widehat{H}_S, \left[\widehat{H}_S, 
\rho_S(t)\right]\right] 
where the intrinsic clock-induced dephasing rate \gamma_{\text{clock}} \sim \frac{\hbar}{\Delta 
E_C} suppresses off-diagonal coherences between energy eigenstates \vert{}E_j\rangle and

\rho_{jk}(t) = \rho_{jk}(0) \, e^{-i(E_j - E_k)t/\hbar} \, \exp\left(-\frac{(E_j - E_k)^2 
\gamma_{\text{clock}} t}{\hbar^2}\right)
